The ensemble as a low-rank covariance

An ensemble Kalman filter holds a finite sample of a density and not the density itself. This chapter fixes how that sample is laid out and how its moments are defined, and then develops the fact everything downstream relies on. The sample covariance of NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ members has rank at most NE−1N_\mathcal{E} - 1NE​−1, so it is fully described by a thin factor and never needs to be formed.

Three things organise the chapter. The rank bound follows from centring, and it also says where an update can and cannot go. Sampling error is what the small rank costs, and it can be measured directly. And ensemble_covariance returns a specific operator, whose abilities and limits are worth knowing exactly.

The ensemble matrix¶

An ensemble of NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ states is a matrix with members as rows:

X=[x(1)⋯x(NE)]⊤∈RNE×Dx.\mat{X} = \begin{bmatrix} \vect{x}^{(1)} & \cdots & \vect{x}^{(N_\mathcal{E})} \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_x}.X=[x(1)​⋯​x(NE​)​]⊤∈RNE​×Dx​.
(2.1)

Axis 0 is the ensemble axis and axis 1 the state axis, so particles[e] is member eee and jax.vmap(fn)(particles) maps a per-state function over the members. Much of the data assimilation literature stacks members as columns, so its formulas appear here transposed.

The mean and the anomalies both come from one centring matrix Π=I−11⊤/NE∈RNE×NE\gmat{\Pi} = \mat{I} - \vect{1} \vect{1}^\top / N_\mathcal{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times N_\mathcal{E}}Π=I−11⊤/NE​∈RNE​×NE​:

xˉ=1NEX⊤1∈RDx,X~=X−1xˉ⊤=ΠX∈RNE×Dx.\bar{\vect{x}} = \frac{1}{N_\mathcal{E}} \mat{X}^\top \vect{1} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}, \qquad \tilde{\mat{X}} = \mat{X} - \vect{1} \bar{\vect{x}}^\top = \gmat{\Pi} \mat{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_x}.xˉ=NE​1​X⊤1∈RDx​,X~=X−1xˉ⊤=ΠX∈RNE​×Dx​.
(2.2)

Π\gmat{\Pi}Π is the orthogonal projector onto the complement of 1\vect{1}1, so 1⊤X~=0\vect{1}^\top \tilde{\mat{X}} = \vect{0}1⊤X~=0. The pair (xˉ,X~)(\bar{\vect{x}}, \tilde{\mat{X}})(xˉ,X~) holds the same information as X\mat{X}X, and most of the analysis algebra is written on it.

The sample covariance¶

The sample covariance divides the anomaly scatter by c=NE−1c = N_\mathcal{E} - 1c=NE​−1:

Σ^x=1cX~⊤X~∈RDx×Dx.\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x = \frac{1}{c} \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \tilde{\mat{X}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}.Σ^x​=c1​X~⊤X~∈RDx​×Dx​.
(2.3)

The divisor makes it unbiased, which a short calculation shows. For members drawn independently with mean μx\gvect{\mu}_xμx​ and covariance Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​, centring on the sample mean gives

∑ex~(e)x~(e)⊤=∑e(x(e)−μx)(x(e)−μx)⊤−NE(xˉ−μx)(xˉ−μx)⊤.\sum_e \tilde{\vect{x}}^{(e)} \tilde{\vect{x}}^{(e)\top} = \sum_e (\vect{x}^{(e)} - \gvect{\mu}_x)(\vect{x}^{(e)} - \gvect{\mu}_x)^\top - N_\mathcal{E} (\bar{\vect{x}} - \gvect{\mu}_x)(\bar{\vect{x}} - \gvect{\mu}_x)^\top.e∑​x~(e)x~(e)⊤=e∑​(x(e)−μx​)(x(e)−μx​)⊤−NE​(xˉ−μx​)(xˉ−μx​)⊤.
(2.4)

The first term has expectation NEΣxN_\mathcal{E} \gmat{\Sigma}_xNE​Σx​ and the second Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​, because V[xˉ]=Σx/NE\mathbb{V}[\bar{\vect{x}}] = \gmat{\Sigma}_x / N_\mathcal{E}V[xˉ]=Σx​/NE​. So E[Σ^x]=Σx\mathbb{E}[\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x] = \gmat{\Sigma}_xE[Σ^x​]=Σx​, while the divisor NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ would be low by the factor c/NEc / N_\mathcal{E}c/NE​. Using ccc is also why filterax needs at least two members.

Rank, and where an update can go¶

Since X~=ΠX\tilde{\mat{X}} = \gmat{\Pi} \mat{X}X~=ΠX and Π\gmat{\Pi}Π has rank NE−1N_\mathcal{E} - 1NE​−1,

rank(Σ^x)=rank(X~)≤min⁡(NE−1,Dx),\op{rank}(\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x) = \op{rank}(\tilde{\mat{X}}) \le \min(N_\mathcal{E} - 1, D_x),rank(Σ^x​)=rank(X~)≤min(NE​−1,Dx​),
(2.5)

however large DxD_xDx​ is. With 50 members and Dx=106D_x = 10^6Dx​=106, the ensemble places all its uncertainty in a 49-dimensional subspace. Any covariance-vector product lands in that subspace, because Σ^xv=X~⊤(X~v)/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x \vect{v} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top (\tilde{\mat{X}} \vect{v}) / cΣ^x​v=X~⊤(X~v)/c is a combination of the rows of X~\tilde{\mat{X}}X~. Every ensemble Kalman increment has this form:

xt∣t(e)−xt∣t−1(e)=X~⊤w(e),w(e)∈RNE.\vect{x}^{(e)}_{t \mid t} - \vect{x}^{(e)}_{t \mid t-1} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \vect{w}^{(e)}, \qquad \vect{w}^{(e)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E}}.xt∣t(e)​−xt∣t−1(e)​=X~⊤w(e),w(e)∈RNE​.
(2.6)

So an update cannot correct an error outside the span of the anomalies, and the filter does not even know such an error exists. Program 2.7 measures both sides of this: how much of an ETKF increment leaves the span, and how much of an unrelated direction lies inside it.

The low-rank factor¶

With the rank bound in hand, the covariance needs only a thin factor:

Σ^x=UxUx⊤,Ux=1cX~⊤∈RDx×NE.\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x = \mat{U}_x \mat{U}_x^\top, \qquad \mat{U}_x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{c}} \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times N_\mathcal{E}}.Σ^x​=Ux​Ux⊤​,Ux​=c​1​X~⊤∈RDx​×NE​.
(2.7)

A covariance-vector product Ux(Ux⊤v)\mat{U}_x (\mat{U}_x^\top \vect{v})Ux​(Ux⊤​v) costs O(NEDx)O(N_\mathcal{E} D_x)O(NE​Dx​), and storing the factor takes NEDxN_\mathcal{E} D_xNE​Dx​ numbers against Dx2D_x^2Dx2​. Adding a structured matrix keeps this useful, because the Woodbury identity then solves against the sum through an NE×NEN_\mathcal{E} \times N_\mathcal{E}NE​×NE​ system. Chapter 4 uses exactly that for the innovation covariance.

Sampling error¶

The sample covariance is unbiased, but each entry is noisy. For Gaussian members, cΣ^xc \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_xcΣ^x​ is Wishart, and each entry has the variance

V[[Σ^x]ij]=1c([Σx]ii[Σx]jj+[Σx]ij2).\mathbb{V}\big[[\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x]_{ij}\big] = \frac{1}{c} \big([\gmat{\Sigma}_x]_{ii} [\gmat{\Sigma}_x]_{jj} + [\gmat{\Sigma}_x]_{ij}^2\big).V[[Σ^x​]ij​]=c1​([Σx​]ii​[Σx​]jj​+[Σx​]ij2​).
(2.8)

A pair of uncorrelated unit-variance components therefore shows a covariance of typical size 1/c1 / \sqrt{c}1/c​, and the same holds for the sample correlation. The Kalman update acts on those values as if they were real, which is the spurious correlation that distance-based filtering suppresses Houtekamer & Mitchell, 1998Hamill et al., 2001.

The eigenvalues show a different symptom. With Σx=I\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \mat{I}Σx​=I every true eigenvalue is 1, but for large DxD_xDx​ and ccc at a fixed ratio β=Dx/c\beta = D_x / cβ=Dx​/c the sample eigenvalues γ^i\hat{\gamma}_iγ^​i​ spread over a known range Marčenko & Pastur, 1967:

(1−β)2≤γ^i≤(1+β)2.\big(1 - \sqrt{\beta}\big)^2 \le \hat{\gamma}_i \le \big(1 + \sqrt{\beta}\big)^2.(1−β​)2≤γ^​i​≤(1+β​)2.
(2.9)

When β>1\beta > 1β>1 the remaining Dx−cD_x - cDx​−c eigenvalues are exactly zero. So a small ensemble is not uniformly unsure. It overstates the variance in a few directions, and it reports no variance at all in the rest.

filterax.cross_covariance returns the dense counterpart Σ^xy=X~⊤H~/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \tilde{\mat{H}} / cΣ^xy​=X~⊤H~/c, a Dx×DyD_x \times D_yDx​×Dy​ array, since DyD_yDy​ is usually small. All four primitives (ensemble_mean, ensemble_anomalies, ensemble_covariance, cross_covariance) are pure functions at Layer 0.

It prints LowRankUpdate DiagonalLinearOperator, (500, 20) (20,) (500,), 10000 250000, 3e-15, 20 True and False. The factor holds 10,000 numbers where the matrix would hold 250,000, and the anomalies sum to zero to round-off. The operator is tagged symmetric but not positive semi-definite, because its zero diagonal base carries no such tag.

It prints ['6e-17', '2e-16', '6e-17', '2e-16', '1e-15'], (50, 25) 4e-16 and 0.999 0.799. The first two lines are round-off. The last averages 20,000 sample variances with NE=5N_\mathcal{E} = 5NE​=5 and a true variance of 1. The divisor ccc gives 0.999 and the divisor NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ gives 0.799, as (2.4) predicts.

Numerics¶

Anomalies are the one place in this chapter where float32 loses digits, because subtracting the mean cancels. A member with ∣xˉ∣|\bar{x}|∣xˉ∣ large against its spread sss keeps only about ϵ∣xˉ∣/s\epsilon |\bar{x}| / sϵ∣xˉ∣/s of relative accuracy in its anomaly, with ϵ\epsilonϵ the machine epsilon. A temperature near 300 with a small spread is the usual case. Program 2.5 measures the relative error against float64, and then subtracts a reference value before the cast.

It prints s = 1.0: raw 3e-05, shifted 4e-08 and s = 0.01: raw 2e-03, shifted 5e-08. A spread of 0.01 about 300 costs a fifth of a percent in the anomalies, close to the estimate ϵ∣xˉ∣/s\epsilon |\bar{x}| / sϵ∣xˉ∣/s. Subtracting a reference value before the cast restores float32 accuracy, and that step is the caller’s, since filterax takes the array it is given. Keeping the factor and not the product helps downstream, since forming UxUx⊤\mat{U}_x \mat{U}_x^\topUx​Ux⊤​ squares any condition number. The analysis routes in chapter 4 and chapter 6 are built around that, and chapter 18 collects the float32 limits.

When it breaks¶

A bare sample covariance cannot be solved. Woodbury needs to invert the base, and here the base is zero. gaussx.solve and gaussx.logdet do not raise on this operator: they return NaN. That holds with NE≤DxN_\mathcal{E} \le D_xNE​≤Dx​, where the matrix is singular, and also with NE>DxN_\mathcal{E} > D_xNE​>Dx​, where it is invertible. Adding a positive definite noise covariance fixes it, and that is how filterax builds the innovation covariance.

It prints nan nan, 4 3, nan nan, 3, 3e-15, 2e-15 and ValueError. The third and fourth values show the invertible case failing all the same, while the operator with a noise base solves to round-off and matches the dense log-determinant. A single member raises a ValueError, because the divisor ccc would be zero. Note also that rank counts the columns of the factor, NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​, while the rank of the matrix is NE−1N_\mathcal{E} - 1NE​−1.

as_matrix is for small problems. On this operator it forms an NE×NEN_\mathcal{E} \times N_\mathcal{E}NE​×NE​ matrix of weights on the way to a Dx×DxD_x \times D_xDx​×Dx​ result. So it needs 8NE28 N_\mathcal{E}^28NE2​ bytes in float64 even for a small state. With very many members and a small state, cross_covariance(particles, particles) gives the same dense matrix without that array.

The span limits what can be learned. Program 2.7 runs an ETKF analysis with NE=20N_\mathcal{E} = 20NE​=20 and Dx=200D_x = 200Dx​=200, and projects vectors onto the rows of the forecast anomalies.

It prints 2e-15 0.905. The increment is inside the span to round-off, as (2.6) says. Random directions have on average the fraction 0.905 of their energy outside it, which matches 1−c/Dx1 - c / D_x1−c/Dx​ with c=19c = 19c=19 and Dx=200D_x = 200Dx​=200. No update can reach that part.

When a low-rank ensemble covariance is the right answer¶

Use ensemble_covariance when you need a covariance as an operator, for matrix-vector products or to pass into gaussx. Use cross_covariance when you need a dense Dx×DyD_x \times D_yDx​×Dy​ array, or a dense covariance from very many members. Use ensemble_anomalies when you are writing an update by hand, since most formulas are simplest on X~\tilde{\mat{X}}X~.

The representation itself suits a large DxD_xDx​ with a covariance that depends on the flow and could never be stored. If DxD_xDx​ is small and the model is linear, keep a real covariance instead, with SquareRootKF (chapter 17). If NE≪DxN_\mathcal{E} \ll D_xNE​≪Dx​, plan on localization (chapter 11) and inflation (chapter 12), because the sampling error above will otherwise reach the analysis.
