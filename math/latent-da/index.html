Latent-space ensemble assimilation

When the state is a high-resolution field, every member needs an expensive forecast. The sample covariance also lives in a space far larger than the dynamics actually explore. If the system evolves near a low-dimensional manifold, a codec can expose it, and the ensemble can be assimilated in those coordinates Peyron et al., 2021Amendola et al., 2021.

Three ideas organise this chapter. The analysis is unchanged: only the observation operator and the forecast are rewired, so every ensemble filter works as it stands. A linear codec gives an exact statement of what this buys and what it loses. And a nonlinear decoder is not free, because it moves nonlinearity into the observation operator, where the update only linearises it.

The construction¶

Take an encoder genc:RDx→RDzg_\text{enc}: \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_z}genc​:RDx​→RDz​ and a decoder gdec:RDz→RDxg_\text{dec}: \mathbb{R}^{D_z} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_x}gdec​:RDz​→RDx​, usually with Dz≪DxD_z \ll D_xDz​≪Dx​ and gdec(genc(x))≈xg_\text{dec}(g_\text{enc}(\vect{x})) \approx \vect{x}gdec​(genc​(x))≈x on the data. The latent ensemble Z∈RNE×Dz\mat{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_z}Z∈RNE​×Dz​ encodes each member, and the two pieces of wiring close the cycle:

z(e)=genc(x(e)),hz=h∘gdec:RDz→RDy.\vect{z}^{(e)} = g_\text{enc}(\vect{x}^{(e)}), \qquad h_z = h \circ g_\text{dec}: \mathbb{R}^{D_z} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_y}.z(e)=genc​(x(e)),hz​=h∘gdec​:RDz​→RDy​.
(15.1)

The lifted operator hzh_zhz​ is what the filter sees. Observations and Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​ stay in observation space, untouched, and an ensemble filter never needs the Jacobian of hzh_zhz​.

The forecast has two forms, which differ in cost. A learned surrogate fzf_zfz​ replaces the expensive model, and the alternative takes the state-space model fff through the codec:

zt=fz(zt−1),zt=genc(f(gdec(zt−1))).\vect{z}_t = f_z(\vect{z}_{t-1}), \qquad \vect{z}_t = g_\text{enc}\big(f(g_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_{t-1}))\big).zt​=fz​(zt−1​),zt​=genc​(f(gdec​(zt−1​))).
(15.2)

The second form saves nothing in the forecast, and the analysis still runs in RDz\mathbb{R}^{D_z}RDz​.

The analysis in latent coordinates¶

Take the update of chapter 6 and substitute Z~\tilde{\mat{Z}}Z~ for X~\tilde{\mat{X}}X~. Each member maps to h(e)=hz(z(e))\vect{h}^{(e)} = h_z(\vect{z}^{(e)})h(e)=hz​(z(e)), and the mean residual rˉ\bar{\vect{r}}rˉ and anomalies H~\tilde{\mat{H}}H~ follow as before. The precision of the weights and the mean weights involve only observation-space quantities,

Λw=cI+H~Σε−1H~⊤,wˉ=Λw−1H~Σε−1rˉ,\gmat{\Lambda}_w = c \mat{I} + \tilde{\mat{H}} \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \tilde{\mat{H}}^\top, \qquad \bar{\vect{w}} = \gmat{\Lambda}_w^{-1} \tilde{\mat{H}} \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \bar{\vect{r}},Λw​=cI+H~Σε−1​H~⊤,wˉ=Λw−1​H~Σε−1​rˉ,
(15.3)

with c=NE−1c = N_\mathcal{E} - 1c=NE​−1, and the analysis applies them to the latent members:

zˉt∣t=zˉ+Z~⊤wˉ,Z~t∣t=WZ~.\bar{\vect{z}}_{t \mid t} = \bar{\vect{z}} + \tilde{\mat{Z}}^\top \bar{\vect{w}}, \qquad \tilde{\mat{Z}}_{t \mid t} = \mat{W} \tilde{\mat{Z}}.zˉt∣t​=zˉ+Z~⊤wˉ,Z~t∣t​=WZ~.
(15.4)

So DzD_zDz​ enters only through the last two products, which cost O(NE2Dz)O(N_\mathcal{E}^2 D_z)O(NE2​Dz​) in place of O(NE2Dx)O(N_\mathcal{E}^2 D_x)O(NE2​Dx​) on the ensemble-space route. The analysis was only ever linear in the state size, so that is not where most of the saving lies. What changes is the rank of the sample covariance, which is at most min⁡(NE−1,Dz)\min(N_\mathcal{E} - 1, D_z)min(NE​−1,Dz​), and the forecast, when a surrogate is available.

A linear codec is an exact reduction¶

Let U∈RDx×Dz\mat{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_z}U∈RDx​×Dz​ have orthonormal columns, with genc(x)=U⊤xg_\text{enc}(\vect{x}) = \mat{U}^\top \vect{x}genc​(x)=U⊤x and gdec(z)=Uzg_\text{dec}(\vect{z}) = \mat{U} \vect{z}gdec​(z)=Uz. Suppose every member lies in the range of U\mat{U}U, so that X=ZU⊤\mat{X} = \mat{Z} \mat{U}^\topX=ZU⊤. Then decoding recovers the members exactly, so H~\tilde{\mat{H}}H~ and rˉ\bar{\vect{r}}rˉ are the same in both spaces, and so is everything in (15.3). Multiplying the mean equation of (15.4) by U\mat{U}U on the left, with UZ~⊤=X~⊤\mat{U} \tilde{\mat{Z}}^\top = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\topUZ~⊤=X~⊤, and the anomaly equation by U⊤\mat{U}^\topU⊤ on the right gives

Xt∣t=Zt∣tU⊤,\mat{X}_{t \mid t} = \mat{Z}_{t \mid t} \mat{U}^\top,Xt∣t​=Zt∣t​U⊤,
(15.5)

which is the state-space ETKF, member for member. In this case latent assimilation is purely a change of coordinates.

The encoded forecast in (15.2) with a linear model F\mat{F}F becomes U⊤FU\mat{U}^\top \mat{F} \mat{U}U⊤FU, a Galerkin projection. That equals the true dynamics on the range of U\mat{U}U only if F\mat{F}F maps that range to itself.

Now suppose the members are not in the range. Write x(e)=Uz(e)+x⊥(e)\vect{x}^{(e)} = \mat{U} \vect{z}^{(e)} + \vect{x}_\perp^{(e)}x(e)=Uz(e)+x⊥(e)​ with U⊤x⊥(e)=0\mat{U}^\top \vect{x}_\perp^{(e)} = \vect{0}U⊤x⊥(e)​=0. The first encode discards x⊥(e)\vect{x}_\perp^{(e)}x⊥(e)​ permanently, and so the codec acts in two ways. Ensemble noise in those directions can no longer use up the NE−1N_\mathcal{E} - 1NE​−1 directions of the update, which is the benefit. Truth in those directions can never be recovered, which is the cost, so any part of xtrue\vect{x}_\text{true}xtrue​ outside the range is an error floor:

∥xˉt∣t−xtrue,t∥2≥∥(I−UU⊤)xtrue,t∥2.\Vert \bar{\vect{x}}_{t \mid t} - \vect{x}_{\text{true},t} \Vert^2 \ge \Vert (\mat{I} - \mat{U} \mat{U}^\top) \vect{x}_{\text{true},t} \Vert^2.∥xˉt∣t​−xtrue,t​∥2≥∥(I−UU⊤)xtrue,t​∥2.
(15.6)

The wiring is thin composition over Layer 1, with no new analysis classes.

A codec is structural: any object with .encode and .decode works, which is also the shape of pipekit_cycle.LatentMap. Note that latent_ensemble and decode_ensemble take a callable, so pass codec.encode, whereas LiftedObs takes the object and calls its .decode. A trained autoencoder and a learned surrogate replace the toys below with nothing else changing.

The running example is a pattern advected around a periodic domain of Dx=100D_x = 100Dx​=100 sites, moving one site per window. The codec is Dz=6D_z = 6Dz​=6 Fourier modes of wavenumbers 1 to 3. In a mode of wavenumber kkk, a shift by δ\deltaδ rotates the sine and cosine coefficients (ak,bk)(a_k, b_k)(ak​,bk​) by the angle kδk \deltakδ:

ak←akcos⁡kδ+bksin⁡kδ,bk←bkcos⁡kδ−aksin⁡kδ.a_k \leftarrow a_k \cos k \delta + b_k \sin k \delta, \qquad b_k \leftarrow b_k \cos k \delta - a_k \sin k \delta.ak​←ak​coskδ+bk​sinkδ,bk​←bk​coskδ−ak​sinkδ.
(15.7)

Every fifth site is observed, so Dy=20D_y = 20Dy​=20, with noise variance 0.25.

It prints 0e+00, 0e+00 and 0.719. With the identity codec, LatentETKF matches ETKF to the last bit, so the wrapper adds wiring and no arithmetic. The last number is the root mean square of the true field.

Algorithm¶

Program 15.2 writes out one latent cycle with the Layer 0 and Layer 1 pieces and a Layer 1 filter. LatentETKF does the same thing inside assimilate.

It prints 0e+00 and 0e+00.

The result is a LatentAssimilationResult. The fields ending in _z are the primary representation, the arrays the filter updated. The other fields are decoded views, so that smoothers, diagnostics and plots work unchanged. The log-likelihoods are computed in observation space, so one array serves both views.

With in_x_space=False, assimilate takes a ready-made NE×DzN_\mathcal{E} \times D_zNE​×Dz​ ensemble and skips the first encode. An inflator runs in latent space because the loop only ever sees Z\mat{Z}Z, so an additive inflator’s covariance must be Dz×DzD_z \times D_zDz​×Dz​.

LatentLETKF has the same body as LatentETKF, with no radius and no localization. Latent dimensions of a typical autoencoder are global modes with no notion of physical distance, so the class exists for wiring parity only.

Agreement with the state-space ETKF¶

(15.5) says a linear orthonormal codec reproduces the state-space ETKF when the members lie in its range. Advection maps Fourier modes to themselves, so the same holds through the forecast, and the three forecast routes must agree.

It prints 5e-15, 1e-14 and 1e-14, all round-off. So in this case the two state sizes give one answer, and the latent run differs only in what it costs.

What the codec buys¶

The reduction holds only when the members are already in the range. Program 15.4 adds white noise of standard deviation 1 to every site of the initial members, which is larger than the true field’s 0.719. The state-space filter must spend its NE−1N_\mathcal{E} - 1NE​−1 directions on it, while the first encode discards it.

It prints 9 6, 0.652 and 0.084. The state-space anomalies have the full rank of NE−1=9N_\mathcal{E} - 1 = 9NE​−1=9 in a space of 100 dimensions, so nothing in the sample covariance tells signal from noise. The latent anomalies span all 6 of their dimensions, which makes this a well-sampled problem, and the error falls by a factor of nearly eight.

Nonlinear decoders and Gaussianity¶

A common argument is that an encoder Gaussianises the state, so the Kalman update fits better in z\vect{z}z than in x\vect{x}x. This is half of the story, because a nonlinear decoder also makes hzh_zhz​ nonlinear, and the update only linearises it over the ensemble.

Program 15.5 tests this on a case with an exact answer. A scalar state has a lognormal prior, x=ezx = e^{z}x=ez with z∼N(0,0.64)z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.64)z∼N(0,0.64), and is observed directly with noise variance 0.25. The prior is Gaussian in zzz, so the log codec is the ideal encoder, and a grid integration gives the true posterior mean.

It prints:

Neither space wins everywhere. Where the observation lies in the bulk of the prior, the log codec is the more accurate and it keeps every member positive. The state-space update gives a negative member at y=0.1y = 0.1y=0.1. Where the observation lies in the prior’s tail, the state-space update is better, because its hhh is linear and the likelihood dominates. The latent update overshoots there, reaching 9.112 against a true 5.839. The cause is that hz=ezh_z = e^{z}hz​=ez is strongly curved, and the update fits one straight line through the prior ensemble.

The table also shows that the two views can disagree. With a nonlinear decoder the mean of the decoded members is not the decoded mean, so state which of the two is being reported.

Gradients through the codec¶

The whole construction is differentiable, so the codec and the surrogate can be trained through the filter (chapter 14) Chen et al., 2023. LatentETKF.assimilate is a Python loop, which jax.grad handles for short windows. For long windows, compose LatentDynamics and LiftedObs with differentiable_assimilate, which runs the same arithmetic in a scan and returns the latent histories only. Its obs_op closes over the decoder, so decoder weights receive a gradient. road_enkf_grad_step differentiates only its dynamics argument, so it can learn the surrogate but not the decoder.

It prints 4e-15, then -527.4808 -527.4808, then 0.0625 0.0628 466.7. The scan matches LatentETKF and the decoder gradient matches its central difference. Forty local steps from a speed of 0.05 land within 1% of the true speed.

When it breaks¶

Whatever the codec cannot represent, the filter cannot correct. Program 15.7 adds a pattern of wavenumber 10 to the truth, which the codec has no mode for, and compares the error with the floor (15.6).

It prints 0.0 0.084 0.000 and 0.5 0.363 0.354. With the pattern present, almost all of the error is the floor, and no number of members or observations can lower it.

Three traps of the wiring are also worth knowing. Program 15.8 measures them.

  • LatentETKF wraps anything that is not an AbstractDynamics in LatentDynamics, so a plain function f(z, t0, t1) fails, although flx.ETKF accepts one.

  • EncodedDynamics preserves the forecast only if genc∘gdecg_\text{enc} \circ g_\text{dec}genc​∘gdec​ is the identity on the ensemble, so a mismatched codec injects spurious dynamics in z\vect{z}z even when fff does nothing.

  • LatentLETKF takes no radius, so it cannot be swapped for LETKF by name.

It prints 'function' object has no attribute 'step', then 3.039, then 0e+00. The first is the error from the wrapped plain function. The second is how far one step of “do nothing” moves z=1\vect{z} = \vect{1}z=1, whose norm is 2.449, so the codec itself is doing the forecasting. The third confirms that LatentLETKF and LatentETKF are the same computation.

When latent assimilation is the right answer¶

Use LatentETKF when the field has a low intrinsic dimension and a small ensemble cannot sample the full state, as in Program 15.4. It pays most when a learned surrogate replaces an expensive forward model, because that is where the large saving lies. It is nearly free when a codec already exists for compression or emulation, and then the codec can be fine-tuned through the filter. A transformation such as the log codec also keeps a constrained variable physical, if observations stay in the bulk of the prior.

Stay in state space when the truth has structure the codec cannot represent, since the floor (15.6) is permanent. Stay there too when localization matters, because LatentLETKF does not localize, and when a curved decoder meets a highly informative observation. With a linear codec, members in its range and an exact model, the two give one answer, so choose by cost.
