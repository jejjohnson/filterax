The stochastic EnKF

The stochastic ensemble Kalman filter Evensen, 1994Burgers et al., 1998Houtekamer & Mitchell, 1998 is the original and simplest member of the family. It computes one Kalman gain from the forecast ensemble and updates every member against its own randomly perturbed copy of the observation.

Three questions organise this chapter. Why do the perturbations have to be there at all? What do they cost at a finite ensemble size? And which of the two routes to the gain does the update take, since that decides what float32 and a singular noise covariance can do?

The perturbed-observation update¶

Take the forecast ensemble X∈RNE×Dx\mat{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_x}X∈RNE​×Dx​ with members as rows, the mapped members h(e)=h(x(e))∈RDy\vect{h}^{(e)} = h(\vect{x}^{(e)}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}h(e)=h(x(e))∈RDy​, the observation y∈RDy\vect{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y∈RDy​ and the noise covariance Σε∈RDy×Dy\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σε​∈RDy​×Dy​. Their anomalies are X~\tilde{\mat{X}}X~ and H~\tilde{\mat{H}}H~, with c=NE−1c = N_\mathcal{E} - 1c=NE​−1, so that Σ^xy=X~⊤H~/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \tilde{\mat{H}} / cΣ^xy​=X~⊤H~/c and Σ^h=H~⊤H~/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_h = \tilde{\mat{H}}^\top \tilde{\mat{H}} / cΣ^h​=H~⊤H~/c. The gain G^∈RDx×Dy\hat{\mat{G}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_y}G^∈RDx​×Dy​ of chapter 4 is computed once:

G^=Σ^xyΣ^y−1,Σ^y=Σ^h+Σε.\hat{\mat{G}} = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_y^{-1}, \qquad \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_y = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_h + \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon.G^=Σ^xy​Σ^y−1​,Σ^y​=Σ^h​+Σε​.
(5.1)

Each member then has its own residual, built from its own draw ε(e)∈RDy\gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}ε(e)∈RDy​:

xt∣t(e)=x(e)+G^(y+ε(e)−h(e)),ε(e)∼N(0,Σε).\vect{x}_{t \mid t}^{(e)} = \vect{x}^{(e)} + \hat{\mat{G}} \left(\vect{y} + \gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)} - \vect{h}^{(e)}\right), \qquad \gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vect{0}, \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon).xt∣t(e)​=x(e)+G^(y+ε(e)−h(e)),ε(e)∼N(0,Σε​).
(5.2)

The observation operator enters only through h(e)\vect{h}^{(e)}h(e), so a nonlinear hhh needs no special handling. The draws are independent across members and across windows. Reusing one is a real error, which Program 5.9 measures.

Leeuwen (2020) argues that the draw belongs to the modelled observation h(e)\vect{h}^{(e)}h(e) rather than to y\vect{y}y. Since −ε(e)-\gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)}−ε(e) has the same distribution, the two readings give the same mean and covariance for a Gaussian prior and likelihood, and the name “perturbed observations” has stuck.

Why the perturbations are needed¶

Stack the draws as rows of E∈RNE×Dy\mat{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_y}E∈RNE​×Dy​, with sample mean εˉ∈RDy\bar{\gvect{\varepsilon}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}εˉ∈RDy​ and anomalies E~\tilde{\mat{E}}E~. Averaging (5.2) over the members, and then subtracting that average, separates the mean from the anomalies:

xˉt∣t=xˉ+G^(rˉ+εˉ),X~t∣t=X~+(E~−H~)G^⊤.\bar{\vect{x}}_{t \mid t} = \bar{\vect{x}} + \hat{\mat{G}} \left(\bar{\vect{r}} + \bar{\gvect{\varepsilon}}\right), \qquad \tilde{\mat{X}}_{t \mid t} = \tilde{\mat{X}} + \left(\tilde{\mat{E}} - \tilde{\mat{H}}\right) \hat{\mat{G}}^\top.xˉt∣t​=xˉ+G^(rˉ+εˉ),X~t∣t​=X~+(E~−H~)G^⊤.
(5.3)

Write Σ^ε=E~⊤E~/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{\varepsilon} = \tilde{\mat{E}}^\top \tilde{\mat{E}} / cΣ^ε​=E~⊤E~/c for the sample covariance of the draws, and Σ^xε=X~⊤E~/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{x\varepsilon} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \tilde{\mat{E}} / cΣ^xε​=X~⊤E~/c and Σ^hε=H~⊤E~/c\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{h\varepsilon} = \tilde{\mat{H}}^\top \tilde{\mat{E}} / cΣ^hε​=H~⊤E~/c for their sample cross-covariances. The sample covariance of the anomalies in (5.3) then expands, for every finite ensemble, into

Σ^t∣t=Σ^x−Σ^xyG^⊤−G^Σ^xy⊤+G^Σ^hG^⊤+G^Σ^εG^⊤+N+N⊤,\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{t \mid t} = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x - \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy} \hat{\mat{G}}^\top - \hat{\mat{G}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy}^\top + \hat{\mat{G}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_h \hat{\mat{G}}^\top + \hat{\mat{G}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{\varepsilon} \hat{\mat{G}}^\top + \mat{N} + \mat{N}^\top,Σ^t∣t​=Σ^x​−Σ^xy​G^⊤−G^Σ^xy⊤​+G^Σ^h​G^⊤+G^Σ^ε​G^⊤+N+N⊤,
(5.4)

where the term N=(Σ^xε−G^Σ^hε)G^⊤∈RDx×Dx\mat{N} = (\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{x\varepsilon} - \hat{\mat{G}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{h\varepsilon}) \hat{\mat{G}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}N=(Σ^xε​−G^Σ^hε​)G^⊤∈RDx​×Dx​ collects the sample correlation between the draws and the forecast.

Take the expectation over the draws with the forecast held fixed. Then E[Σ^ε]=Σε\mathbb{E}[\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{\varepsilon}] = \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonE[Σ^ε​]=Σε​ and E[N]=0\mathbb{E}[\mat{N}] = \vect{0}E[N]=0. The gain satisfies G^Σ^y=Σ^xy\hat{\mat{G}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_y = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy}G^Σ^y​=Σ^xy​, so the last two surviving terms combine into Σ^xyG^⊤\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy} \hat{\mat{G}}^\topΣ^xy​G^⊤, and what remains is the Kalman covariance:

E[Σ^t∣t]=Σ^x−G^Σ^xy⊤=Σ^x∣y.\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{t \mid t}\right] = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x - \hat{\mat{G}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xy}^\top = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{x \mid y}.E[Σ^t∣t​]=Σ^x​−G^Σ^xy⊤​=Σ^x∣y​.
(5.5)

This holds with the sample covariances, so it needs no linear hhh and no large ensemble. For a linear H\mat{H}H the same statement is the familiar identity (I−G^H)Σ^x(I−G^H)⊤+G^ΣεG^⊤=(I−G^H)Σ^x(\mat{I} - \hat{\mat{G}} \mat{H}) \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x (\mat{I} - \hat{\mat{G}} \mat{H})^\top + \hat{\mat{G}} \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \hat{\mat{G}}^\top = (\mat{I} - \hat{\mat{G}} \mat{H}) \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x(I−G^H)Σ^x​(I−G^H)⊤+G^Σε​G^⊤=(I−G^H)Σ^x​.

Now remove the draws. Setting E=0\mat{E} = \mat{0}E=0 in (5.4) leaves the same terms without Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​, so

Σ^t∣tunperturbed=Σ^x∣y−G^ΣεG^⊤.\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{t \mid t}^\text{unperturbed} = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{x \mid y} - \hat{\mat{G}} \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \hat{\mat{G}}^\top.Σ^t∣tunperturbed​=Σ^x∣y​−G^Σε​G^⊤.
(5.6)

The unperturbed update is therefore too confident at every analysis, by exactly the term the observation noise should contribute, and the shortfall compounds over cycles. That is why gaussx offers no perturb=False flag: the deterministic alternative is a different filter (chapter 6), and not an option on this one.

The price: sampling noise¶

(5.5) holds only in expectation, and one analysis takes one draw. The mean in (5.3) is off the Kalman mean by exactly G^εˉ\hat{\mat{G}} \bar{\gvect{\varepsilon}}G^εˉ. Since εˉ∼N(0,Σε/NE)\bar{\gvect{\varepsilon}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vect{0}, \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon / N_\mathcal{E})εˉ∼N(0,Σε​/NE​), its size has a closed form:

E[∥G^εˉ∥22]=1NEtr(G^ΣεG^⊤).\mathbb{E}\left[\left\Vert \hat{\mat{G}} \bar{\gvect{\varepsilon}} \right\Vert_2^2\right] = \frac{1}{N_\mathcal{E}} \op{tr}\left(\hat{\mat{G}} \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \hat{\mat{G}}^\top\right).E[∥∥​G^εˉ∥∥​22​]=NE​1​tr(G^Σε​G^⊤).
(5.7)

The covariance is off through Σ^ε−Σε\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{\varepsilon} - \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣ^ε​−Σε​ and through N\mat{N}N in (5.4), and both shrink as 1/NE1 / \sqrt{N_\mathcal{E}}1/NE​​. This noise comes on top of the sampling error the forecast ensemble already carries. The deterministic square-root filters of chapters 6 and 7 have neither term.

Implementation in filterax¶

There are three layers, and each is useful on its own.

  • gaussx.enkf_analysis is the update itself. It takes either a key or a prebuilt perturbed_obs, and it exposes localization, obs_localization, dense_innovation and solver.

  • filterax.filters.StochasticEnKF is the Layer 1 analysis step. It maps (particles, obs, obs_op, obs_noise) to an AnalysisResult, and of those gaussx options it passes on only key.

  • filterax.StochasticEnKF is the Layer 2 cycle of chapter 16, which derives the key of window ttt as jr.fold_in(base_key, t).

filterax.perturbed_observations draws the stack y+ε(e)\vect{y} + \gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)}y+ε(e) through gaussx.sample_mvn, while gaussx.enkf_analysis draws its own with a factor LL⊤=Σε\mat{L} \mat{L}^\top = \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonLL⊤=Σε​. Both have the right distribution, but they produce the same draws for a given key only when Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​ is diagonal: with Toeplitz or low-rank noise the two routes give different members.

The example has NE=30N_\mathcal{E} = 30NE​=30 members of a state with Dx=4D_x = 4Dx​=4, drawn around 1 with standard deviation 0.5, and observes components 0 and 2 with noise variance 0.1.

It prints [0.43 0.528 0.531 0.522] [0.256 0.518 0.294 0.523] and [0.289 0.491 0.272 0.492] -3.401. The observed components contract and the others move only through their sample correlation with them. A second key gives a different spread each time, where the ETKF of chapter 6 gets the single Kalman value [0.255 0.516 0.272 0.511].

result.log_likelihood is the Gaussian evidence of the mean residual under Σ^y\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_yΣ^y​, the same number the ETKF reports on these inputs, and it does not depend on the draw.

Agreement with the Kalman update¶

Three checks follow from the algebra above. Given the same draws, the production route must match Program 5.1. With no draws, the covariance must match (5.6) exactly. And averaged over many draws, the mean and covariance must approach the Kalman values, by (5.5).

It prints 2e-16 0e+00 2e-16, then the two diagonals [0.0648 0.2662 0.0737 0.2608] and [0.0228 0.2617 0.0193 0.2569], then 1.0e-03 2.7e-04. The first line is round-off, and it shows that key and perturbed_obs use the same draws for this diagonal noise. The diagonals show what the unperturbed ensemble loses on the observed components. The last line is Monte Carlo error over 4,000 draws, not a bias.

Which route, and float32¶

The two routes of the table solve the same system, and in float64 they agree to round-off. They do not agree in float32 when observations are strong or their variances span many decades. The Woodbury form subtracts two nearly equal large terms when Σε−1\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}Σε−1​ is large, and float32 loses the difference.

Program 5.5 measures the gain in float32 against a float64 dense solve.

Differentiating the update¶

The update has no eigendecomposition, so the trouble of chapter 6 does not arise. With the key held fixed, the draw is a differentiable function of the noise through ε(e)=Ln(e)\gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)} = \mat{L} \vect{n}^{(e)}ε(e)=Ln(e) with fixed n(e)\vect{n}^{(e)}n(e), so (5.2) is differentiable as written.

It prints 76.584646 76.584646. The gradient is that of one realisation, so a training loss built on it inherits the sampling noise above. Fixing the key across training steps fits to that one draw. Chapter 14 discusses the alternatives.

When it breaks¶

Small ensembles¶

The sampling noise adds to the ordinary weakness of a small ensemble, which is that it underestimates its own error. Program 5.8 runs the stochastic EnKF and the ETKF on the Lorenz-96 twin of chapter 6, with Dx=40D_x = 40Dx​=40 and every second site observed with unit variance Lorenz, 2006. Neither filter has inflation or localization.

It prints:

NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​Stochastic RMSEStochastic spreadETKF RMSEETKF spread

603.520.230.300.26

The ETKF is healthy at all three sizes, and the stochastic EnKF is worse at each. At 60 members it has diverged, with the error fifteen times the spread, although 40 members did not. So these runs sit close to its divergence threshold, and they depend on the draws. Inflation and localization (chapter 12, chapter 11) treat this for both filters.

The last print, 5e-15, shows that the Layer 2 model and the compiled loop above agree over five windows.

A repeated key¶

analysis(..., key=None) falls back to the key on the filter, so a hand-written loop that never passes a key reuses one draw every window. Each member then sees the same perturbed observation again and again, and it converges to that value instead of to the posterior. Program 5.9 makes this exact: 100 members of a static state with prior variance 1 assimilate 50 windows of unit-variance observations, so the true posterior variance is 1/511 / 511/51.

It prints [0.019 0.017 0.027] with fresh keys, [0.712 0.966 1.156] with one key, and 0.020 as the true value. With a repeated key, the spread stays near the noise variance however many observations arrive. Layer 2 is not exposed to this, because it folds the window index into its key.

Non-Gaussian priors¶

(5.2) is a Gaussian update in whatever coordinates the state is given. For a non-Gaussian prior it is biased, and the bias does not shrink with ensemble size. The transform of chapter 6 has the same bias, though the two filters shape the ensemble differently Lawson & Hansen, 2004.

For one observation of one variable, the ETKF multiplies the anomalies by a constant, so it keeps the shape of the prior exactly. The stochastic update convolves the prior with Gaussian draws, which moves its shape towards a Gaussian. Program 5.10 measures this on a lognormal prior, with importance weights on the forecast members as the reference.

It prints the skewnesses 1.842 1.842 0.506 0.528, for the prior, the ETKF, the stochastic EnKF and the importance-weighted reference. The second line gives the means 1.057 1.055 0.979, in the same order without the prior. Both Kalman updates share nearly the same mean error, which is the bias of the Gaussian assumption. The ETKF keeps all of the prior’s skewness, the stochastic EnKF ends close to the reference here, and neither has the right mean. The remedy for the bias is a change of coordinates, which the gaussx.enkf_analysis docstring discusses, and not a choice between these two filters.

When the stochastic EnKF is the right answer¶

Use filters.StochasticEnKF when you want the simplest correct reference, with one gain and one line of update. It also suits an ensemble large enough that 1/NE1 / \sqrt{N_\mathcal{E}}1/NE​​ noise does not matter. It is also natural when many members meet few observations, since the dense route costs only O(NEDy2+Dy3)O(N_\mathcal{E} D_y^2 + D_y^3)O(NE​Dy2​+Dy3​) for the gain and has no ensemble-space matrix. Its randomness is what the ensemble Kalman processes of chapter 9 build on, so it is the direct bridge to them.

Reach for a square-root filter when the ensemble is small, since Program 5.8 shows the price, or when you want a reproducible analysis with no key to manage. Reach for the tapered gain of chapter 11 when you need the stochastic update with localization, through gaussx.enkf_analysis(..., localization=...), because filters.StochasticEnKF does not pass tapers on.
