Worked example: a Lorenz-96 twin experiment

A twin experiment is how an ensemble filter is judged honestly: simulate a truth, observe it with noise, assimilate, and compare the analysis with the truth you hid. This chapter runs one end to end on the Lorenz-96 model Lorenz & Emanuel, 1998, which is chaotic and small enough to run in seconds.

Three things organise it. Small ensembles need treating, and the treatments are inflation, localization, or both. The honest report is RMSE against spread over several independent twins, including the settings that fail, because a filter that works on one seed may not work on the next. And a smoother pass on top is not automatically an improvement.

The model and the twin¶

The state is xt∈RDx\vect{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}xt​∈RDx​, with Dx=40D_x = 40Dx​=40 sites on a circle. filterax has no dynamical core, so the model is written out here. With indices taken cyclically and forcing 8, its tendency at site kkk is

dxkdt=(xk+1−xk−2)xk−1−xk+8,\frac{\mathrm{d} x_k}{\mathrm{d} t} = (x_{k+1} - x_{k-2}) x_{k-1} - x_k + 8,dtdxk​​=(xk+1​−xk−2​)xk−1​−xk​+8,
(19.1)

and a window advances the state by one fourth-order Runge-Kutta step of Δt=0.05\Delta t = 0.05Δt=0.05. The twin observes every second site, Dy=20D_y = 20Dy​=20, with unit noise:

yt=Hxt+εt,εt∼N(0,I).\vect{y}_t = \mat{H} \vect{x}_t + \gvect{\varepsilon}_t, \qquad \gvect{\varepsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\vect{0}, \mat{I}).yt​=Hxt​+εt​,εt​∼N(0,I).
(19.2)

The first ensemble is the true initial state plus Gaussian noise of variance 4, with NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ members as rows of X∈RNE×Dx\mat{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_x}X∈RNE​×Dx​. Every run has T=300T = 300T=300 windows, and the scores use the last 200, once the filter has settled. Two scales judge the result: the observation noise of 1, and the standard deviation of the state itself.

It prints 3.67, the standard deviation of the truth. An RMSE near that value means the filter has lost the trajectory and knows nothing beyond the attractor. An RMSE below 1 means the filter beats the observations at the observed sites, and it must also fill in the unobserved half.

One run, end to end¶

The Layer 2 class filterax.ETKF composes a dynamics function, the analysis and an optional inflator, and loops over windows in Python. Its form of the RTPS inflator Whitaker & Hamill, 2012 relaxes each site’s spread back toward its forecast value,

σktarget=(1−α)σkt∣t+ασkt∣t−1,\sigma^\text{target}_k = (1 - \alpha) \sigma^{t \mid t}_k + \alpha \sigma^{t \mid t-1}_k,σktarget​=(1−α)σkt∣t​+ασkt∣t−1​,
(19.3)

for α∈[0,1]\alpha \in [0, 1]α∈[0,1], by rescaling the anomalies at site kkk and leaving the mean alone.

A Python loop is fine for one run and costly for a sweep, since every window dispatches its own operations. differentiable_assimilate runs the same cycle as one jax.lax.scan, so it can be compiled once and vectorised over twins.

It prints 3e-14, so the two loops are the same computation, and the sweep below uses the scan.

ETKF, RTPS and LETKF against each other¶

Program 19.3 runs eight independent twins for each setting, with the same truth and different observation noise and first ensembles. It calls a twin healthy if its RMSE stays under the observation noise of 1. For the healthy twins it reports the median RMSE and spread, and otherwise the worst RMSE and any NaN.

The LETKF Hunt et al., 2007 needs coordinates, and it measures distance in Euclidean terms. So the sites sit on a circle of circumference 40, with the observations at their sites, which makes distance respect the cyclic domain. The distance is the straight chord between two points on the circle, so the radius ℓ\ellℓ is close to a count of sites when it is small: a chord of 4 spans about 4.07 sites of arc, and a chord of 8 about 8.65. The LETKF drops observations beyond it.

Four things stand out.

Without inflation or localization, 6 of 8 twins diverge at 20 members. The two that survive have an RMSE of 0.38 against a spread of 0.25, so they are overconfident as well.

Inflation alone has a narrow window at this size. At α=0.5\alpha = 0.5α=0.5 every twin survives, with RMSE and spread nearly equal. But α=0.3\alpha = 0.3α=0.3 loses one twin, α=0.7\alpha = 0.7α=0.7 loses three, and α=0.9\alpha = 0.9α=0.9 loses all of them. Choosing α\alphaα from a single seed would be unsafe here, because 0.3 gives the lowest RMSE of the three and also fails once.

Localization alone is enough at 20 members, with the lowest RMSE here and every twin healthy. The spread of 0.28 is a little under the RMSE of 0.32, and adding RTPS at α=0.2\alpha = 0.2α=0.2 brings it to 0.35, at the same RMSE.

At 10 members a wide radius is not enough alone, as the LETKF at ℓ=8\ell = 8ℓ=8 without inflation fails 6 of 8. A tighter radius recovers 7 of 8, and inflation with the wide radius recovers all 8, at an RMSE of 0.33. The global ETKF with RTPS at α=0.3\alpha = 0.3α=0.3 fails on all 8.

What the failures look like¶

The NaN deserves a closer look, because nothing raises it. RTPS at α=0.9\alpha = 0.9α=0.9 restores almost all the forecast spread after every analysis. On the LETKF at the same α\alphaα no twin goes non-finite, though none stays healthy either, with a worst RMSE of 1.35, so the global analysis matters too. Program 19.4 follows the first global twin to its first non-finite member. It also repeats the best LETKF with its sites on a line, where sites 0 and 39 look 39 apart.

A smoother pass¶

A smoother uses later observations to refine earlier states. EnKS takes the forecast and analysis histories and walks back over all the windows, and FixedLagSmoother stops each backward walk after lag steps. With X~t∣t\tilde{\mat{X}}_{t \mid t}X~t∣t​ and X~t+1∣t\tilde{\mat{X}}_{t+1 \mid t}X~t+1∣t​ the analysis and forecast anomalies, one backward step on the members is

Xt∣T=Xt∣t+(Xt+1∣T−Xt+1∣t)X~t+1∣t+X~t∣t,\mat{X}_{t \mid T} = \mat{X}_{t \mid t} + (\mat{X}_{t+1 \mid T} - \mat{X}_{t+1 \mid t}) \tilde{\mat{X}}_{t+1 \mid t}^{+} \tilde{\mat{X}}_{t \mid t},Xt∣T​=Xt∣t​+(Xt+1∣T​−Xt+1∣t​)X~t+1∣t+​X~t∣t​,
(19.4)

where +^{+}+ is the pseudo-inverse. The sample gain here has no localization, whichever filter produced the history.

Program 19.5 scores the windows from 100 to 250, so that every window has later data to use. It reports medians over the eight twins, because one diverged twin dominates a mean.

A lag of 10 windows helps, taking the RMSE from about 0.32 to about 0.21, a third lower. Longer lags give that back, and the full EnKS is worse than no smoother on all three filters. The spread is no guide either: on the LETKF it keeps falling with the lag, to 0.033 against an RMSE of 0.364. A likely cause is that sample cross-covariances over many windows of a chaotic model are mostly noise. The unlocalized gain of (19.4) still acts on them, though we have not isolated this.

So on this problem use FixedLagSmoother at about 10 windows, and read its spread as a lower bound on the error.

When this setup is the right answer¶

Start from the LETKF with light RTPS on a spatially extended chaotic model with few members. It was healthy on every twin here, down to 10 members. Use the global ETKF only when the ensemble is large against the error directions of the model, which chapter 6 shows at 40 members on this problem. Tune on several twins, and report the failures with the scores.
