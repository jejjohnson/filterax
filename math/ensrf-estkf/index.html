EnSRF and ESTKF: the square-root family

The ETKF of chapter 6 is one member of a family of deterministic square-root filters Tippett et al., 2003. Each of them reproduces the Kalman analysis covariance on the sample statistics without perturbed observations, and the square-root idea itself goes back to Andrews (1968). The members differ in where the square root is taken and in whether the observations arrive as one batch or one at a time.

This chapter covers the three filters filterax ships alongside ETKF, and one relation organises it. The batch EnSRF and the ESTKF are the ETKF under other names, and in filterax they run the same code. EnSRF_Serial is different code and takes a product of one-observation ETKFs, so it has the same mean and covariance with different members.

The batch EnSRF: mean from the gain¶

Whitaker & Hamill (2002) phrase the update in the most Kalman-familiar way, by treating the mean and the anomalies separately. With X~\tilde{\mat{X}}X~ and H~\tilde{\mat{H}}H~ the anomalies in RNE×Dx\mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_x}RNE​×Dx​ and RNE×Dy\mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times D_y}RNE​×Dy​, rˉ=y−hˉ\bar{\vect{r}} = \vect{y} - \bar{\vect{h}}rˉ=y−hˉ the mean residual and c=NE−1c = N_\mathcal{E} - 1c=NE​−1, the mean takes the gain and the anomalies take a square root:

xˉt∣t=xˉ+G^rˉ,X~t∣t=WX~,W=(cΣw)1/2.\bar{\vect{x}}_{t \mid t} = \bar{\vect{x}} + \hat{\mat{G}} \bar{\vect{r}}, \qquad \tilde{\mat{X}}_{t \mid t} = \mat{W} \tilde{\mat{X}}, \qquad \mat{W} = (c \gmat{\Sigma}_w)^{1/2}.xˉt∣t​=xˉ+G^rˉ,X~t∣t​=WX~,W=(cΣw​)1/2.
(7.1)

Here Σw\gmat{\Sigma}_wΣw​ is the weight covariance of chapter 6, and W\mat{W}W is its symmetric transform. The push-through identity of that chapter gives G^=X~⊤ΣwH~Σε−1\hat{\mat{G}} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_w \tilde{\mat{H}} \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}G^=X~⊤Σw​H~Σε−1​, so the gain form of the mean and the ETKF’s weight form are one expression. The batch EnSRF and the ETKF are therefore the same filter, and only the phrasing differs. filters.EnSRF calls the same internal function as filters.ETKF, and it keeps its name for people who think of the update this way.

The serial EnSRF: one observation at a time¶

The classical distinction appears when observations are assimilated one by one. Let k∈{1,…,Dy}k \in \{1, \dots, D_y\}k∈{1,…,Dy​} index the observations, each with a variance σε,k2=[Σε]kk\sigma_{\varepsilon,k}^2 = [\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon]_{kk}σε,k2​=[Σε​]kk​. At step kkk, let h~k∈RNE\tilde{\vect{h}}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E}}h~k​∈RNE​ be column kkk of the mapped anomalies of the current ensemble, and rˉk\bar{r}_krˉk​ the scalar residual. The sample variance of the mapped members, the innovation variance and the gain are

σ^h,k2=h~k⊤h~kc,σ^y,k2=σ^h,k2+σε,k2,g^k=X~⊤h~kc σ^y,k2,\hat{\sigma}_{h,k}^2 = \frac{\tilde{\vect{h}}_k^\top \tilde{\vect{h}}_k}{c}, \qquad \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}^2 = \hat{\sigma}_{h,k}^2 + \sigma_{\varepsilon,k}^2, \qquad \hat{\vect{g}}_k = \frac{\tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \tilde{\vect{h}}_k}{c \, \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}^2},σ^h,k2​=ch~k⊤​h~k​​,σ^y,k2​=σ^h,k2​+σε,k2​,g^​k​=cσ^y,k2​X~⊤h~k​​,
(7.2)

with g^k∈RDx\hat{\vect{g}}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}g^​k​∈RDx​, and no matrix inverse anywhere. The mean takes the full gain, and the anomalies take a reduced one, scaled by βk∈(1/2,1)\beta_k \in (1/2, 1)βk​∈(1/2,1):

xˉ←xˉ+g^krˉk,X~←X~−βkh~kg^k⊤.\bar{\vect{x}} \leftarrow \bar{\vect{x}} + \hat{\vect{g}}_k \bar{r}_k, \qquad \tilde{\mat{X}} \leftarrow \tilde{\mat{X}} - \beta_k \tilde{\vect{h}}_k \hat{\vect{g}}_k^\top.xˉ←xˉ+g^​k​rˉk​,X~←X~−βk​h~k​g^​k⊤​.
(7.3)

Why the gain is reduced¶

The factor βk\beta_kβk​ follows from asking for the Kalman covariance. Dropping the index and writing Σ^xh=X~⊤h~/c=σ^y2g^\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xh} = \tilde{\mat{X}}^\top \tilde{\vect{h}} / c = \hat{\sigma}_y^2 \hat{\vect{g}}Σ^xh​=X~⊤h~/c=σ^y2​g^​, the anomalies in (7.3) have sample covariance

Σ^x−(2βσ^y2−β2σ^h2)g^g^⊤.\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x - \left(2 \beta \hat{\sigma}_y^2 - \beta^2 \hat{\sigma}_h^2\right) \hat{\vect{g}} \hat{\vect{g}}^\top.Σ^x​−(2βσ^y2​−β2σ^h2​)g^​g^​⊤.
(7.4)

The Kalman covariance is Σ^x−g^Σ^xh⊤=Σ^x−σ^y2g^g^⊤\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x - \hat{\vect{g}} \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{xh}^\top = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_x - \hat{\sigma}_y^2 \hat{\vect{g}} \hat{\vect{g}}^\topΣ^x​−g^​Σ^xh⊤​=Σ^x​−σ^y2​g^​g^​⊤. So β\betaβ must solve σ^h2β2−2σ^y2β+σ^y2=0\hat{\sigma}_h^2 \beta^2 - 2 \hat{\sigma}_y^2 \beta + \hat{\sigma}_y^2 = 0σ^h2​β2−2σ^y2​β+σ^y2​=0. With σ^h2=σ^y2−σε2\hat{\sigma}_h^2 = \hat{\sigma}_y^2 - \sigma_\varepsilon^2σ^h2​=σ^y2​−σε2​, the root below one is

β=σ^yσ^y+σε=11+σε2/σ^y2.\beta = \frac{\hat{\sigma}_y}{\hat{\sigma}_y + \sigma_\varepsilon} = \frac{1}{1 + \sqrt{\sigma_\varepsilon^2 / \hat{\sigma}_y^2}}.β=σ^y​+σε​σ^y​​=1+σε2​/σ^y2​​1​.
(7.5)

The full gain β=1\beta = 1β=1 is the unperturbed update of chapter 5, which is short by σε2g^g^⊤\sigma_\varepsilon^2 \hat{\vect{g}} \hat{\vect{g}}^\topσε2​g^​g^​⊤. Reducing the gain is the deterministic way to supply that missing spread.

Each step is a one-observation ETKF¶

Substituting g^k\hat{\vect{g}}_kg^​k​ into (7.3) shows that the anomalies are multiplied on the left by a symmetric matrix:

X~←WkX~,Wk=I−βkc σ^y,k2h~kh~k⊤.\tilde{\mat{X}} \leftarrow \mat{W}_k \tilde{\mat{X}}, \qquad \mat{W}_k = \mat{I} - \frac{\beta_k}{c \, \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}^2} \tilde{\vect{h}}_k \tilde{\vect{h}}_k^\top.X~←Wk​X~,Wk​=I−cσ^y,k2​βk​​h~k​h~k⊤​.
(7.6)

Its eigenvalues are 1 on the complement of h~k\tilde{\vect{h}}_kh~k​, and on h~k\tilde{\vect{h}}_kh~k​ itself they are 1−βkσ^h,k2/σ^y,k2=σε,k/σ^y,k1 - \beta_k \hat{\sigma}_{h,k}^2 / \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}^2 = \sigma_{\varepsilon,k} / \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}1−βk​σ^h,k2​/σ^y,k2​=σε,k​/σ^y,k​.

The ETKF with this one observation has Λw=cI+h~kh~k⊤/σε,k2\gmat{\Lambda}_w = c \mat{I} + \tilde{\vect{h}}_k \tilde{\vect{h}}_k^\top / \sigma_{\varepsilon,k}^2Λw​=cI+h~k​h~k⊤​/σε,k2​. Its transform cΛw−1/2\sqrt{c} \gmat{\Lambda}_w^{-1/2}c​Λw−1/2​ has eigenvalue 1 on the complement, and on h~k\tilde{\vect{h}}_kh~k​ the precision eigenvalue is cσ^y,k2/σε,k2c \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}^2 / \sigma_{\varepsilon,k}^2cσ^y,k2​/σε,k2​, which gives σε,k/σ^y,k\sigma_{\varepsilon,k} / \hat{\sigma}_{y,k}σε,k​/σ^y,k​ again. So Wk\mat{W}_kWk​ is exactly the symmetric ETKF transform for one observation, and g^krˉk\hat{\vect{g}}_k \bar{r}_kg^​k​rˉk​ is its mean update.

A full sweep is then a product,

X~t∣t=WDy⋯W2W1X~.\tilde{\mat{X}}_{t \mid t} = \mat{W}_{D_y} \cdots \mat{W}_2 \mat{W}_1 \tilde{\mat{X}}.X~t∣t​=WDy​​⋯W2​W1​X~.
(7.7)

Each factor fixes 1\vect{1}1, since h~k⊤1=0\tilde{\vect{h}}_k^\top \vect{1} = 0h~k⊤​1=0, so the product is mean-preserving as chapter 6 requires. But a product of symmetric matrices is not symmetric in general, so this transform is not the batch W\mat{W}W. By the sequential form of Bayes’ rule with diagonal noise, its mean and covariance still equal the batch values for a linear hhh. Only the members differ, and they depend on the order of the sweep.

ESTKF: the error subspace¶

Nerger et al. (2012) observe that the ETKF’s NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​-dimensional weight space is one dimension too big, because 1⊤X~=0\vect{1}^\top \tilde{\mat{X}} = \vect{0}1⊤X~=0. The ESTKF works in the complement of 1\vect{1}1. Choose L∈RNE×(NE−1)\mat{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} \times (N_\mathcal{E} - 1)}L∈RNE​×(NE​−1) with L⊤L=I\mat{L}^\top \mat{L} = \mat{I}L⊤L=I and L⊤1=0\mat{L}^\top \vect{1} = \vect{0}L⊤1=0, which implies LL⊤=I−11⊤/NE\mat{L} \mat{L}^\top = \mat{I} - \vect{1} \vect{1}^\top / N_\mathcal{E}LL⊤=I−11⊤/NE​. The reduced anomalies and reduced precision are

XL=L⊤X~,HL=L⊤H~,ΛL=cI+HLΣε−1HL⊤,\mat{X}_L = \mat{L}^\top \tilde{\mat{X}}, \qquad \mat{H}_L = \mat{L}^\top \tilde{\mat{H}}, \qquad \gmat{\Lambda}_L = c \mat{I} + \mat{H}_L \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \mat{H}_L^\top,XL​=L⊤X~,HL​=L⊤H~,ΛL​=cI+HL​Σε−1​HL⊤​,
(7.8)

with XL∈Rc×Dx\mat{X}_L \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times D_x}XL​∈Rc×Dx​, HL∈Rc×Dy\mat{H}_L \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times D_y}HL​∈Rc×Dy​ and ΛL∈Rc×c\gmat{\Lambda}_L \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times c}ΛL​∈Rc×c. The analysis uses the mean weights wˉL∈Rc\bar{\vect{w}}_L \in \mathbb{R}^cwˉL​∈Rc and the symmetric root WL∈Rc×c\mat{W}_L \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times c}WL​∈Rc×c:

wˉL=ΛL−1HLΣε−1rˉ,WL=(cΛL−1)1/2,\bar{\vect{w}}_L = \gmat{\Lambda}_L^{-1} \mat{H}_L \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \bar{\vect{r}}, \qquad \mat{W}_L = (c \gmat{\Lambda}_L^{-1})^{1/2},wˉL​=ΛL−1​HL​Σε−1​rˉ,WL​=(cΛL−1​)1/2,
(7.9)
xˉt∣t=xˉ+XL⊤wˉL,X~t∣t=LWLXL.\bar{\vect{x}}_{t \mid t} = \bar{\vect{x}} + \mat{X}_L^\top \bar{\vect{w}}_L, \qquad \tilde{\mat{X}}_{t \mid t} = \mat{L} \mat{W}_L \mat{X}_L.xˉt∣t​=xˉ+XL⊤​wˉL​,X~t∣t​=LWL​XL​.
(7.10)

Why it equals the ETKF¶

Since H~⊤1=0\tilde{\mat{H}}^\top \vect{1} = \vect{0}H~⊤1=0, the vector 1\vect{1}1 is an eigenvector of Λw\gmat{\Lambda}_wΛw​ with eigenvalue ccc, and L\mat{L}L spans the remaining ones. So the precision splits as

Λw=cNE11⊤+LΛLL⊤,\gmat{\Lambda}_w = \frac{c}{N_\mathcal{E}} \vect{1} \vect{1}^\top + \mat{L} \gmat{\Lambda}_L \mat{L}^\top,Λw​=NE​c​11⊤+LΛL​L⊤,
(7.11)

and any spectral function splits in the same way, ϕ(Λw)=ϕ(c)11⊤/NE+Lϕ(ΛL)L⊤\phi(\gmat{\Lambda}_w) = \phi(c) \vect{1} \vect{1}^\top / N_\mathcal{E} + \mat{L} \phi(\gmat{\Lambda}_L) \mat{L}^\topϕ(Λw​)=ϕ(c)11⊤/NE​+Lϕ(ΛL​)L⊤. Applied to X~\tilde{\mat{X}}X~ the first term vanishes, because 1⊤X~=0\vect{1}^\top \tilde{\mat{X}} = \vect{0}1⊤X~=0, which gives WX~=LWLL⊤X~\mat{W} \tilde{\mat{X}} = \mat{L} \mat{W}_L \mat{L}^\top \tilde{\mat{X}}WX~=LWL​L⊤X~ and the same result for the mean. This is not an approximation, and it is the sense in which the two filters are the same analysis. So filters.ESTKF builds no L\mat{L}L and decomposes no c×cc \times cc×c matrix. It runs the ETKF route of chapter 6, including its rank-DyD_yDy​ shortcut, and keeps the name for people coming from the error-subspace literature.

Algorithm¶

Program 7.1 writes out two forms the production code does not contain as such. They are the reduced ESTKF, with L\mat{L}L from a Householder reflection, and the serial sweep with its explicit step transforms.

Implementation in filterax¶

All of these share the analysis(particles, obs, obs_op, obs_noise) signature, so comparing them is one line each. filters.EnSRF, filters.ESTKF and filters.EnSRF_Serial are Layer 1 analysis steps. Layer 2 has filterax.EnSRF for the cycle of chapter 16, and no cycle class for ESTKF or EnSRF_Serial, so those run in a hand-written loop such as Program 7.6.

The example has NE=30N_\mathcal{E} = 30NE​=30 members with Dx=4D_x = 4Dx​=4, and three linear observations with different noise variances.

It prints True, True, 4e-16 1e-16 0.028 and -4.358415 -4.358415. EnSRF and ESTKF return the ETKF array bit for bit. EnSRF_Serial matches the mean and the covariance to round-off, and its members differ. All four report the same evidence, computed from the forecast ensemble before the sweep.

Agreement with the references¶

Three identities above can be measured: (7.10) against the production ESTKF, (7.7) against the production serial sweep, and the claim that one serial step is a one-observation ETKF. The last check compares the anomalies through the explicit transform, and reverses the sweep to see the order dependence.

It prints 2e-15, 2e-15, 0.009, 7e-16, 9e-16 and 2e-16 3e-17 0.059. The reduced form and the written-out sweep match the production filters to round-off. The product transform is not symmetric, by 0.009, and it still fixes 1\vect{1}1. One serial step is the one-observation ETKF, member for member. Reversing the order keeps the mean and the covariance and moves the members.

Numerics¶

Float32¶

The serial sweep never forms a matrix with the whole range of Σε−1\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}Σε−1​ in it, because each step meets one variance alone. The ensemble-space route of the batch filters does form one, and that is its accuracy limit in float32 when Dy≥NED_y \ge N_\mathcal{E}Dy​≥NE​ (gaussx#785). Program 7.4 measures the mean increment and the analysis covariance against a float64 dense solve, with observations processed in the given order.

It prints:

With Dy<NED_y < N_\mathcal{E}Dy​<NE​ both are accurate, since the batch filters then take the rank-DyD_yDy​ route. With Dy≥NED_y \ge N_\mathcal{E}Dy​≥NE​ and a wide range of variances, EnSRF_Serial holds float32 accuracy where the ETKF route does not. For a diagonal noise covariance, that makes it the practical answer to that limit when float64 is not an option.

Gradients¶

The serial sweep has no eigendecomposition, so it has no repeated eigenvalue to worry about. Its derivative is that of a chain of rational functions and scalar square roots. Differentiating with respect to a zero noise variance is the one point to avoid, where σε2\sqrt{\sigma_\varepsilon^2}σε2​​ in (7.5) has an infinite slope. The batch filters use the closed-form derivative of chapter 6.

It prints 9.223319 9.223319 and 9.219315 9.219315. The two filters give different values because their members differ, and each loss is a function of the members.

When it breaks¶

These filters share the limits of chapter 6: the update stays in the span of the forecast anomalies, and a small ensemble underestimates its own error. Removing the sampling noise does not cure that. Program 7.6 runs the Lorenz-96 twin of that chapter Lorenz, 2006, with Dx=40D_x = 40Dx​=40, every second site observed with unit variance, and no inflation or localization.

It prints:

Once the ensemble is big enough, the two scores nearly coincide. At 20 members both diverge, with error far above spread, and the different members lead them to different chaotic trajectories.

Two more limits belong to the serial form.

Correlated noise is refused, and whitening is the route around that. Processing one component of a correlated y\vect{y}y at a time would be wrong, so EnSRF_Serial raises NotImplementedError for anything but a bare lx.DiagonalLinearOperator. With Σε=LεLε⊤\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = \mat{L}_\varepsilon \mat{L}_\varepsilon^\topΣε​=Lε​Lε⊤​, assimilating Lε−1y\mat{L}_\varepsilon^{-1} \vect{y}Lε−1​y through Lε−1h\mat{L}_\varepsilon^{-1} hLε−1​h with unit variances is the same problem.

A nonlinear hhh makes the order matter. The production code re-evaluates hhh on the running ensemble, so each step takes a fresh statistical linearisation. That is a different approximation from the batch one, and it depends on the order of the sweep.

It prints NotImplementedError, 3e-16 6e-17 0.019, 2e-02 3e-02 0.103 and 1e-01 2e-02 0.134. The whitened sweep matches the batch filter with the correlated covariance, to round-off in the mean and the covariance. Under the nonlinear map, the serial mean differs from the batch mean by 2e-02 and from its own reversed sweep by 1e-01. So here the order matters more than the choice of serial over batch.

When each is the right answer¶

Use ETKF, EnSRF or ESTKF as your default deterministic batch filter, and pick the name your community uses, since the analysis is the same. They take any structured noise covariance, and ESTKF is the natural name when cross-checking against error-subspace experiments such as those run with PDAF.

Use EnSRF_Serial in three cases:

  • The noise is diagonal, Dy≥NED_y \ge N_\mathcal{E}Dy​≥NE​ and float32 is required, by Program 7.4.

  • Observations arrive incrementally, so that each one can be assimilated as it comes.

  • You want no eigendecomposition anywhere in the derivative.

Use ETKF_Livings of chapter 6 for long runs where the deterministic transform leaves outlying members. None of these replaces the treatments for a small ensemble: use chapter 8 and chapter 12 for those.
