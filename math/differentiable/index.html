Differentiable assimilation

A filter turns a model and a stream of observations into a likelihood of those observations, so it can train the model. Every filterax analysis is a pure JAX computation, so jax.grad of that likelihood reaches dynamics parameters, decoder weights, noise amplitudes and the initial ensemble. The question is not whether the gradient exists but which gradient to pay for.

Three ideas organise this chapter. The exact gradient is a sum over products of cycle Jacobians, which shows exactly what each cheaper strategy throws away. The strategies differ in what they store and what they drop, and the memory they use can be measured rather than assumed. And the analysis contracts perturbations, so the gradient through a filter behaves very differently from the gradient through a free-running chaotic model.

The loss¶

Let θ∈RDθ\gvect{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_\theta}θ∈RDθ​ hold everything being learned. One cycle applies the forward model to each member and then the analysis, including any inflator, written here as a map aaa:

Xt∣t−1=f(Xt−1∣t−1;θ),Xt∣t=a(Xt∣t−1,yt;θ).\mat{X}_{t \mid t-1} = f(\mat{X}_{t-1 \mid t-1}; \gvect{\theta}), \qquad \mat{X}_{t \mid t} = a(\mat{X}_{t \mid t-1}, \vect{y}_t; \gvect{\theta}).Xt∣t−1​=f(Xt−1∣t−1​;θ),Xt∣t​=a(Xt∣t−1​,yt​;θ).
(14.1)

Both ensembles are NE×DxN_\mathcal{E} \times D_xNE​×Dx​. The evidence factorises over windows as p(y1:T)=∏tp(yt∣y1:t−1)p(\vect{y}_{1:T}) = \prod_t p(\vect{y}_t \mid \vect{y}_{1:t-1})p(y1:T​)=∏t​p(yt​∣y1:t−1​), and the forecast ensemble gives each factor a Gaussian approximation (chapter 13). The training loss is its negative logarithm:

L(θ)=∑t=1TLt(θ),Lt=12rˉt⊤Σ^y,t−1rˉt+12log⁡det⁡Σ^y,t+Dy2log⁡2π.\mathcal{L}(\gvect{\theta}) = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathcal{L}_t(\gvect{\theta}), \qquad \mathcal{L}_t = \frac{1}{2} \bar{\vect{r}}_t^\top \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{y,t}^{-1} \bar{\vect{r}}_t + \frac{1}{2} \log \det \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{y,t} + \frac{D_y}{2} \log 2 \pi.L(θ)=t=1∑T​Lt​(θ),Lt​=21​rˉt⊤​Σ^y,t−1​rˉt​+21​logdetΣ^y,t​+2Dy​​log2π.
(14.2)

Here rˉt∈RDy\bar{\vect{r}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}rˉt​∈RDy​ and Σ^y,t∈RDy×Dy\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_{y,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σ^y,t​∈RDy​×Dy​ are the mean residual and innovation covariance of the forecast ensemble Xt∣t−1\mat{X}_{t \mid t-1}Xt∣t−1​, and result.log_likelihoods[t] is −Lt-\mathcal{L}_t−Lt​. This is a principled target for three kinds of θ\gvect{\theta}θ:

  • Physical constants of the forward model, observation-operator gains and error-covariance amplitudes, where descent on L\mathcal{L}L replaces hand-tuning.

  • A neural surrogate in an equinox module, trained to predict the next observation from the filtered state Chen et al., 2022.

  • The weights of a latent codec (chapter 15).

The exact gradient¶

Stack the analysis ensemble into a vector with vec\op{vec}vec, and define its sensitivity to the parameters, St=∂vec(Xt∣t)/∂θ∈RNEDx×Dθ\mat{S}_t = \partial \op{vec}(\mat{X}_{t \mid t}) / \partial \gvect{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} D_x \times D_\theta}St​=∂vec(Xt∣t​)/∂θ∈RNE​Dx​×Dθ​. One cycle has two partial derivatives, taken with Xt−1∣t−1\mat{X}_{t-1 \mid t-1}Xt−1∣t−1​ and θ\gvect{\theta}θ held in turn:

Jt=∂vec(Xt∣t)∂vec(Xt−1∣t−1),Dt=∂vec(Xt∣t)∂θ,\mat{J}_t = \frac{\partial \op{vec}(\mat{X}_{t \mid t})}{\partial \op{vec}(\mat{X}_{t-1 \mid t-1})}, \qquad \mat{D}_t = \frac{\partial \op{vec}(\mat{X}_{t \mid t})}{\partial \gvect{\theta}},Jt​=∂vec(Xt−1∣t−1​)∂vec(Xt∣t​)​,Dt​=∂θ∂vec(Xt∣t​)​,
(14.3)

with Jt∈RNEDx×NEDx\mat{J}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} D_x \times N_\mathcal{E} D_x}Jt​∈RNE​Dx​×NE​Dx​ and Dt∈RNEDx×Dθ\mat{D}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} D_x \times D_\theta}Dt​∈RNE​Dx​×Dθ​. The chain rule gives a recursion for the sensitivity, and the same two partials of the window loss, gt=∂Lt/∂θ∈RDθ\vect{g}_t = \partial \mathcal{L}_t / \partial \gvect{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_\theta}gt​=∂Lt​/∂θ∈RDθ​ and vt=∂Lt/∂vec(Xt−1∣t−1)∈RNEDx\vect{v}_t = \partial \mathcal{L}_t / \partial \op{vec}(\mat{X}_{t-1 \mid t-1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\mathcal{E} D_x}vt​=∂Lt​/∂vec(Xt−1∣t−1​)∈RNE​Dx​, give the gradient:

St=JtSt−1+Dt,∇θL=∑t=1T(gt+St−1⊤vt).\mat{S}_t = \mat{J}_t \mat{S}_{t-1} + \mat{D}_t, \qquad \nabla_{\gvect{\theta}} \mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( \vect{g}_t + \mat{S}_{t-1}^\top \vect{v}_t \right).St​=Jt​St−1​+Dt​,∇θ​L=t=1∑T​(gt​+St−1⊤​vt​).
(14.4)

With a fixed initial ensemble S0=0\mat{S}_0 = \mat{0}S0​=0, and unrolling the recursion shows the products that matter:

St=∑s=1t(JtJt−1⋯Js+1)Ds.\mat{S}_t = \sum_{s=1}^{t} \left( \mat{J}_t \mat{J}_{t-1} \cdots \mat{J}_{s+1} \right) \mat{D}_s.St​=s=1∑t​(Jt​Jt−1​⋯Js+1​)Ds​.
(14.5)

Each term is the effect of θ\gvect{\theta}θ at window sss, carried forward to window ttt. Every strategy in this chapter keeps a different subset of these terms, and whether the dropped terms matter depends on the size of those products.

Take a linear model F\mat{F}F and a linear observation operator H\mat{H}H, and hold the gain Gt\mat{G}_tGt​ fixed. The part of Jt\mat{J}_tJt​ acting on the ensemble mean then follows from xˉt∣t=Fxˉt−1∣t−1+Gt(yt−HFxˉt−1∣t−1)\bar{\vect{x}}_{t \mid t} = \mat{F} \bar{\vect{x}}_{t-1 \mid t-1} + \mat{G}_t (\vect{y}_t - \mat{H} \mat{F} \bar{\vect{x}}_{t-1 \mid t-1})xˉt∣t​=Fxˉt−1∣t−1​+Gt​(yt​−HFxˉt−1∣t−1​):

∂xˉt∣t∂xˉt−1∣t−1=(I−GtH)F.\frac{\partial \bar{\vect{x}}_{t \mid t}}{\partial \bar{\vect{x}}_{t-1 \mid t-1}} = (\mat{I} - \mat{G}_t \mat{H}) \mat{F}.∂xˉt−1∣t−1​∂xˉt∣t​​=(I−Gt​H)F.
(14.6)

The model F\mat{F}F may expand, but I−GtH\mat{I} - \mat{G}_t \mat{H}I−Gt​H removes the observed part of a perturbation at every cycle. This is the error propagator of the filter, and a filter that is tracking its observations keeps it contractive.

Implementation in filterax¶

The Layer 2 models (chapter 16) loop over windows in Python, which jax.grad differentiates, but the trace grows with TTT. differentiable_assimilate runs the same cycle in one jax.lax.scan, with observations and times stacked on a leading axis. Its result is the same AssimilationResult, and the gradient policy is the adjoint= argument. road_enkf_loss_and_grad is a separate driver that accumulates local gradients of the dynamics.

The example learns a scalar in a linear model, f(x;θ)=θxf(\vect{x}; \theta) = \theta \vect{x}f(x;θ)=θx, where the truth has θ=0.95\theta = 0.95θ=0.95. It has NE=12N_\mathcal{E} = 12NE​=12 members and Dx=4D_x = 4Dx​=4 components, of which Dy=2D_y = 2Dy​=2 are observed for T=8T = 8T=8 windows with noise variance 0.1.

It prints 3.854 4.782 and 34.163289 34.163289. The loss is lower at the true θ=0.95\theta = 0.95θ=0.95, the gradient at θ=1\theta = 1θ=1 points towards it, and it matches the central difference.

Algorithm: local gradients by hand¶

ROAD-EnKF Chen et al., 2023 jointly learns a latent surrogate and a decoder inside an ensemble Kalman filter. For long windows it splits the sequence into short subsequences and backpropagates only within each, following truncated backpropagation through time. road_enkf_loss_and_grad is the limit of that idea with subsequences of one window, and it differentiates the dynamics only. Each window sees the previous analysis as a constant, which keeps gt\vect{g}_tgt​ and drops St−1⊤vt\mat{S}_{t-1}^\top \vect{v}_tSt−1⊤​vt​ from (14.4):

∇^θL=∑t=1Tgt.\widehat{\nabla}_{\gvect{\theta}} \mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \vect{g}_t.∇θ​L=t=1∑T​gt​.
(14.7)

Program 14.2 writes this out with a dense loss (14.2), so that it checks both the estimator and the likelihood the filter reports.

It prints 4.782471 14.383752, then 2e-15 1e-14, then 3e-15. The hand loop agrees with the library to round-off, in both the loss and the estimator. The loss is the exact L\mathcal{L}L of Program 14.1, because only the backward pass differs. The local gradient of 14.38 is well under half the exact 34.16.

The adjoint strategies¶

The adjoint= argument takes three strategies, whose names are shared with pipekit_cycle.adjoints; a structurally identical spec from that package is accepted too. Forward values are identical under all of them.

  • DirectAdjoint, the default, stores every step and computes (14.4) exactly.

  • RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint wraps the scan body in jax.checkpoint, so each step stores its inputs and recomputes the rest on the way back, and the gradient is exact Griewank & Walther, 2000.

  • TruncatedAdjoint(k) cuts the carry between windows with stop_gradient, for every window with t≤T−kt \le T - kt≤T−k.

The checkpoints field of RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint is carried but ignored, since the code applies one jax.checkpoint per step and has no schedule. The older checkpoint=True flag selects the same strategy, with a DeprecationWarning.

The truncating cut sets St=0\mat{S}_t = \mat{0}St​=0 for t≤T−kt \le T - kt≤T−k in (14.4), and it does not touch the per-window outputs. So two consequences follow, and neither is a sliding window of depth kkk. A loss summed over the history keeps gt\vect{g}_tgt​ at every window, but only the windows with t≥T−k+2t \ge T - k + 2t≥T−k+2 see any cross-window term. So TruncatedAdjoint(1) on the summed loss is (14.7) exactly, and window ttt gains cross-window terms only once kkk reaches T−t+2T - t + 2T−t+2. A loss on the final ensemble depends on ST\mat{S}_TST​, and there the cut keeps the last kkk terms of (14.5).

The cost of a reverse pass is whatever the scan stores for the way back, and XLA can report it. Only the local driver is flat in TTT, because it finishes each window’s backward pass before starting the next. RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint is linear in TTT with a smaller slope. Its 4.8 kB per window is exactly one ensemble of NEDx=600N_\mathcal{E} D_x = 600NE​Dx​=600 floats of 8 bytes, which is the step input that jax.checkpoint must keep. A recursive schedule in the style of Griewank & Walther (2000) would stop that growth, but the code does not implement one. TruncatedAdjoint(1) costs the same as DirectAdjoint, even on this final-state loss with only one cycle of gradient. The cut is a runtime jnp.where inside the scan body, so the scan still stores every step. So its value is the bounded depth of the gradient and not its memory. The docstrings that promise O(T)O(\sqrt{T})O(T​) and O(1)O(1)O(1) overstate what these two strategies do today.

The other saving of the scan is at trace time. Program 14.5 counts the operations JAX traces for each loop, including those inside the scan’s body.

Numerics: chaos and the filter¶

The exact adjoint of a free-running chaotic model grows like eλmaxTΔte^{\lambda_\text{max} T \Delta t}eλmax​TΔt, with λmax\lambda_\text{max}λmax​ the leading Lyapunov exponent and Δt\Delta tΔt the time per window Lea et al., 2000. That makes the gradient useless over long windows, and it is often quoted as a reason to truncate a filter’s gradient. But (14.6) says the analysis changes the picture, because Jt\mat{J}_tJt​ includes the contraction. Program 14.6 measures it on Lorenz-96 with Dx=20D_x = 20Dx​=20 sites and the forcing θ=8\theta = 8θ=8 as the parameter. It compares the free run with a filter of NE=25N_\mathcal{E} = 25NE​=25 members, multiplicative inflation of 1.02 and unit noise variance. The filter observes every second or every fifth site.

It prints free 100 -286.9 and free 400 903801938.6 for the free run. For the filter over 400 windows it prints 2 -14.0 with every second site observed and 5 401.9 with every fifth.

The free run gains more than six orders of magnitude between T=100T = 100T=100 and T=400T = 400T=400, as the Lyapunov argument predicts. The filter does not. With half the sites observed, the exact gradient of a loss summed over 400 windows has magnitude 14, because each analysis contracts what the model expands. So chaos alone is no reason to truncate a filter’s gradient. The growth returns when the contraction weakens. With one site in five observed, the exact gradient reaches 401.9.

What is refused, and what is not¶

Two ingredients inject PRNG draws, which jax.grad would treat as constants, so the gradient would be that of one arbitrary noise realisation. differentiable_assimilate and road_enkf_loss_and_grad both refuse them with a ValueError at call time:

  • Among the filters, StochasticEnKF (chapter 5) and ETKF_Livings (chapter 6), so use ETKF, EnSRF, ESTKF, EnSRF_Serial or LETKF.

  • Among the inflators, AdditiveInflator (chapter 12), so use MultiplicativeInflator, RTPS or RTPP.

The check looks only for those three classes, so a stochastic filter or inflator of your own passes it silently.

The inflation parameters of the built-in inflators are static fields, so eqx.filter_grad over one sees no leaves and they cannot be trained as module fields. To learn one, write a small AbstractInflator with the parameter as an array field and call the primitive, as in Program 14.9.

The analysis itself is safe. The textbook ETKF has a repeated eigenvalue whose eigh derivative is NaN, and chapter 6 shows how gaussx removes it.

It prints one refusal per ingredient, then -0.848903 -0.848903, then 0. The trainable factor matches its central difference, and the built-in inflator has no leaves for a gradient to reach.

When it breaks¶

The gradient is only as healthy as the filter around it, and five limits are worth knowing.

  • A filter that has stopped tracking loses the contraction of (14.6), and the exact gradient grows, as the sparse case of Program 14.6 shows.

  • The local gradient converges to its own zero and not to the minimum of L\mathcal{L}L, as Program 14.7 shows.

  • road_enkf_loss_and_grad differentiates the dynamics only, so decoder weights, noise amplitudes and inflation factors need differentiable_assimilate.

  • TruncatedAdjoint saves no memory today, and its summed-loss gradient does not improve monotonically with kkk.

  • RTPS gives a NaN gradient when a component has zero forecast spread, even though the forward pass stays finite.

The last of these is cheap to demonstrate. Holding one component constant across the ensemble, as a masked or pinned variable would be, is enough.

It prints 4.813 nan and 4.806 19.830. RTPS takes a standard deviation per component, whose derivative is infinite at zero variance, and RTPP has no such step. So keep RTPS out of a training loop when any component can be pinned, or use RTPP.

When a differentiable filter is the right answer¶

Use differentiable_assimilate with DirectAdjoint when the windows are short enough to store, since its gradient is exact and nothing needs tuning. RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint gives the same gradient for a constant-factor saving, about five times in Program 14.4, so reach for it when DirectAdjoint only just fails to fit.

Use road_enkf_grad_step when memory must stop growing with TTT, the dynamics are what is being learned, and a small bias in the optimum is acceptable.

Use TruncatedAdjoint to bound the depth of a gradient on something road_enkf_loss_and_grad cannot reach, such as a decoder. For a summed loss on the dynamics alone, TruncatedAdjoint(1) computes the same gradient as the local driver at far higher memory.

Reach for something else when the forward model cannot be differentiated: the ensemble Kalman processes of chapter 9 need only forward evaluations. For a short experiment with no training at all, the Layer 2 loop of chapter 16 is simpler.
